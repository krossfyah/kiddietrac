# Assigning educators to rooms

**Staff → Room assignments.** One person at a time: pick them, see their rooms, done.

## Why it matters

Several screens work room by room — who sees which children, and whose attendance and
sign-out figures are counted against them. Without an assignment those screens fall back
to showing the whole centre, which is why an educator can see more children than they
teach, and why the daily digest scorecard reports sign-outs per centre rather than per
person.

## Using it

1. **Choose the person** from the dropdown. Their current rooms are shown straight away.
2. **Edit.** The screen is **read-only until you press it** — these decisions control who
   can see which children, and opening the screen is not consent to change them.
3. Tick the rooms they work in.
4. **Save.** **Cancel** puts it back exactly as it was when you pressed Edit.

Switching to a different person mid-edit asks first.

## Every room in the agency is offered

The list is not limited to the person's own centre. Their own centres are **marked and
sorted to the top**, so reaching outside them is visibly deliberate rather than
impossible.

This is not a loosening. Somebody working across several sites could not be assigned
correctly at all before — the offer was one centre's rooms while they held roles at nine —
and the save has always been validated against the whole agency regardless.

## Worth knowing

- Saving **replaces** a person's rooms rather than adding to them. The row shows the
  complete set, so what you submit is the complete answer.
- Re-saving without changing anything writes nothing, so the record of who assigned a room
  and when is not churned.
- A person can hold several rooms, at several centres.
- In a home-childcare agency, where the provider is also the room, the centre name is not
  repeated on the room — it would just say the same thing twice.
- A banner counts how many people still have no room at all. That number is worth getting
  to zero.
