# HR: lateness, absence score and leave clashes

Three attendance insights in the KiddieTrac HR add-on. An agency admin sets the rules in **Human Resources > HR settings > Attendance rules**; directors see the results for their own centres.

## Lateness

Each person's first clock-in of a scheduled shift is compared with the shift start on the **Schedule**. Anything more than the grace time (5 minutes unless your agency changes it) counts as late. Coming back from a break, and days with no shift, are not compared.

Open **Human Resources > Absence & lateness > Lateness** to see, for any date range: how many clock-ins were compared, how many were late, the total and average minutes, the worst one, the on-time percentage and a pattern (for example *Mostly Mondays*). The ⋮ menu on a row shows each clock-in against its shift. **Export to Excel** downloads the list.

If your agency turns on **Tell the centre's directors**, the centre's directors get a notice when someone clocks in late.

## Absence score

The **Absence score** column in **Human Resources > Absence & lateness** adds weight to frequent short absences: number of absences × number of absences × days off, over the last 12 months (this is the Bradford formula). Three one-day absences score 27; one three-day absence scores 3. Absences joined by only a weekend or a closed day count as one.

By default it counts sick days. Your agency can choose the leave types, the period and the threshold (50 by default). Anyone at or above the threshold is marked; **Only above the absence score** shows just them. The score is a prompt for a conversation, never an automatic decision.

## Leave clashes and minimum staff

When someone asks for time off, the form shows how many others at their centre are already off or asking for those days (never who). The person approving sees who, in the approve window and in the approval email.

An agency admin can set a **minimum staff on a working day** for each centre. The approver is then told which days would go below it. Your agency chooses whether that is a warning or stops the approval. Leave that needs no approval, such as sick days, is never refused, only shown.
