# Giving medications safely

Administering medication is one of the highest-stakes things you do. Kiddietrac gives you a clear record of what's authorised and what's been given — but the record supports your care, it doesn't replace your training or your centre's policy. **Always follow your centre's medication procedure.**

## Before you give anything

1. **Check there's authorisation.** A medication should have a parent/guardian authorisation on file — the medication name, dose, timing, and dates. If it's not authorised, don't give it; talk to your director.
2. **Check the five rights:** right child, right medication, right dose, right time, right route.
3. **Check for allergies** and any conflicting alerts on the child's record.

## Recording a dose

When you administer a dose, log it right away:

1. Open **Medications** and find the child.
2. Record the **dose given**, the **time**, and your name (you're signed in, so it's attributed to you).
3. Add a note if anything was unusual (child spat some out, gave with food, etc.).

Logging immediately — not at the end of the day — is what makes the record trustworthy. It also prevents a second educator from accidentally double-dosing.

## Reading the medication list

The Medications area shows, per child:

- What's authorised, with dose and schedule
- What's already been given today, and when
- What's still due

A quick look tells you whether a lunchtime dose has been handled or is still waiting.

## Safety rules that never change

- **Never give an unauthorised medication**, even at a parent's verbal request — it needs to be on file.
- **Never guess a dose.** If the instruction is unclear, stop and check with your director and the parent.
- **Store medication securely**, out of children's reach, per your centre's policy.
- **Watch for reactions** after giving, especially a first dose, and know your emergency procedure.

## If something goes wrong

A wrong dose, a missed dose, or a reaction is an incident. Follow your centre's procedure, care for the child first, notify the director and parent, and file an **incident report** (see **When something happens**). Honesty and speed protect the child — never hide a mistake.

## Common questions

**"A parent handed me medicine at drop-off with no authorisation."**
Ask them to complete the authorisation (your director can set it up). Until it's on file and within policy, don't administer it.

**"The schedule says a dose is due but the child is asleep / just ate."**
Use your judgement within the authorisation and policy, note the actual time you gave it, and flag anything unusual to the director.

**"I logged the wrong time / dose."**
Tell your director — an accurate record matters, and they can help correct it properly rather than you overwriting it silently.
