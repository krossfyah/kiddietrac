# Issuing awards and certificates

Awards are a small thing that means a lot. A certificate for a kind act, a new skill, or a great week gives a child a moment of pride and gives parents a window into what you noticed — often something no photo could capture.

## Giving an award

1. Open **Awards** (or find the option on a child's card).
2. Pick the **child**.
3. Choose the **type / occasion** — for example a daily "kindness" award, a weekly "helper", or a monthly milestone.
4. Add a short, specific reason.
5. Save.

The parent is notified, the award appears in the child's record, and a **printable certificate** is generated automatically.

## Make it specific

The reason is the best part. Compare:

**Lovely:**
> "For welcoming our new friend Sam all week — you showed him where everything goes and sat with him at lunch. That's real kindness."

**Forgettable:**
> "Good week!"

Specific awards mean more to the child, land better with parents, and become a genuine record of growth over time.

## Ideas for what to recognise

- A **first** — first time sharing, using the toilet, writing their name
- **Kindness** — helping, comforting, including someone
- **Effort** — sticking with something hard
- **A skill** — cutting, climbing, counting, a new word
- **Being a helper** — tidying, leading circle time

You don't need a grand reason. Catching the small good things is exactly the point.

## Keep it fair and frequent

- Spread awards around — over a few weeks, every child should have their moment.
- It's fine for awards to be small and regular rather than rare and grand.
- Tie them to real moments, not favourites.

## Where they end up

- **Parents** get a notification and can open/print the certificate.
- The award stays in the child's **Awards** history, building a lovely record across the year.
- Certificates print cleanly with the child's name and the occasion — great for the fridge or a portfolio.

## Common questions

**"Can I award more than one child at once?"**
Awards are given per child so the reason can be personal — but there's nothing stopping you recognising several children for the same activity, each with their own note.

**"A parent didn't see the certificate."**
Check they have notifications on; the certificate is always saved in the child's Awards section for them to open any time.

**"How often should I give them?"**
There's no rule — follow your centre's approach. Regular, specific, and fair beats rare and generic.
