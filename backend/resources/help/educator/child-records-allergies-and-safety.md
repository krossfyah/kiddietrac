# Child records, allergies and safety

Every child has a record that puts the things you *must* know — allergies, medical alerts, who can collect them — one tap away. Knowing how to read it fast is a core safety skill.

## Opening a child's record

From your **Children** list (or a roster card), tap a child to open their record. It opens as a normal screen, so the app's navigation stays right where you expect it.

## What's in the record

The record leads with **safety**, because that's what you reach for mid-room:

- **⚠️ Allergies** — highlighted in red. Know these before any food or activity.
- **🩺 Health alerts & medical notes** — asthma, seizures, conditions to watch for.
- **🍎 Dietary needs** — restrictions and preferences.
- **Parents / guardians** — with tap-to-call buttons.
- **Emergency contacts** — everyone the family has named, with a **Call** button. The ones marked **may collect** are allowed to pick the child up.
- **Log history** — the child's care and attendance trail: who did what, and when.

Safety cards stay open by default; the rest you can collapse to keep the record scannable.

On the full child record, **Pick-ups & emergency contacts** lists the same people, authorised pick-ups first. Each one shows **✓ can pick up** or **emergency contact only**, their phone numbers (a second number shows its type, such as work, with any extension), and **🚸 pick-up login** if they have their own login to check the children in and out.

## The emergency card

On a child's record, **🆘 Emergency card** puts everything for an emergency on one page:
allergies and medical notes, parents and emergency contacts with their numbers, and who may
collect the child. **Print** opens it ready to print.

You can open the emergency cards of the children **in your own rooms**, the same children you
see on your roster. Every time a card is opened, it is recorded in the audit log.

## Allergies: the golden rule

**Check allergies before every meal, snack, cooking activity, and craft that involves food or common allergens.** A child's allergy card is red for a reason. If you're covering an unfamiliar room, make reading each child's allergies your very first job.

If a child has a severe allergy, know where their emergency medication is and your centre's response plan **before** you need it.

## Pickup: who is allowed

At pickup, the person collecting must be a **guardian or someone marked as allowed to collect**. The record shows exactly who that is. If someone arrives who isn't on the list — or you're unsure — do not release the child; check the record and call the director or the guardian.

The first time a new pick-up person comes, ask for photo ID.

## When a family changes the pick-up list

Parents keep their own emergency contacts and pick-up people in their app. Whenever they add, change or remove someone, the educators of that child's room get a notification — **🚗 Pick-up list addition**, **change** or **removal** — saying who changed what. Tap it to open the child's record.

Read these when they arrive, especially a removal: the person may no longer collect the child.

## Keeping records accurate

You generally **view** these records rather than edit them — allergies and medical details are managed by the director, and the pick-up list by the family and the director. If you notice something out of date or wrong (a parent mentions a new allergy, a pick-up person has changed), tell your director promptly so they can update it, or ask the parent to update their pick-up list in the app. Don't rely on memory for safety-critical facts.

## Privacy

A child's record is confidential. You can see the children in your care; you shouldn't be looking up children who aren't yours, and you should never share a child's medical or family details outside the team. If the app ever shows you a child who isn't in your centre, screenshot it and tell your director — that's a bug.

## Common questions

**"I can't open a child's record — it says access denied."**
You can only see children in your centre/room. If it's a child you should have access to, ask your director to check your room assignment.

**"A parent told me about a new allergy."**
Take it seriously immediately, tell your director to add it to the record, and treat the child as allergic in the meantime.

**"The pickup list looks out of date."**
Flag it to the director before pickup time, or ask the parent to update **Family & pick-ups** in their app — never guess when it comes to releasing a child.
