Website subscribers become a managed list, with a real unsubscribe record

The Subscribers tab printed marketing-leads.jsonl - an append-only log. It cannot say
whether somebody has since unsubscribed and carries no id to act on, so there was nothing
to manage. Unsubscribes were worse: a flat array of email addresses in a JSON file, with no
date, no source, and no way to tell a person who left last year from one who left this
morning.

site_subscribers holds them properly. A subscriber is never deleted on unsubscribe - the
row stays and gains a timestamp, because "when did they unsubscribe" is the question that
actually gets asked, and in several jurisdictions it is what you must be able to show.
Deleting is a separate, deliberate action for genuine erasure, and the confirmation says so.

Both write paths feed it. A website signup is recorded as a subscriber, and re-submitting
the form after leaving counts as opting back in. The public unsubscribe link now stamps the
table as well as the file - and an address that was never on the list still gets a row,
because the request itself is the record.

The file-based suppression list is kept in step rather than replaced: the mail layer reads
it, and quietly moving where suppression lives would be an excellent way to start emailing
people who asked not to be emailed. Verified in a round trip - unsubscribing adds the
address to that file, re-subscribing removes it.

The Website section gains an Unsubscribed tab showing who left, when, and whether they did
it themselves or an administrator did it for them. Row actions are plain buttons; kt-row-
actions folds them into the kebab on its own.

Backfilled from the signup file and from CRM leads marked as coming from the website: two
real subscribers. Five addresses were skipped as test data - probe+N@example.invalid, left
behind by my own throttle testing earlier. .invalid is reserved precisely so it can never
be a real mailbox, and importing those would have put fictional people on a mailing list.
